Repair or replace an automatic door? Five Hong Kong site criteria

When a commercial automatic door breaks down, deciding between repairing the fault and replacing the operator can be tough. Use these five practical criteria from real Hong Kong sites to make a clear, cost-effective call.

Prepared by Hwa Lai EngineeringUpdated 26 November 20265 minute read
Decision guideRepair isolated wear parts; replace the overhead operator once an 8–10+ year unit keeps failing

Faults in sensors, belts, rollers, or exit switches are typically fast and economical to repair. When an older operator suffers repeated motor or controller failures, upgrading the overhead drive unit while keeping your existing glass doors is usually smarter than repeated emergency call-outs.

In busy Hong Kong shops, clinics, and commercial lobbies, an out-of-service automatic door immediately affects air-conditioning, customer flow, and building operations. Most managers want an honest technical view — neither oversold a full replacement when a sensor is at fault, nor trapped paying for recurring repairs on an exhausted motor. Here are the five factors we check on site.

1. Separate peripheral wear parts from core drive-unit failure

An automatic door combines a core operator (motor, control board, power supply) with external and wear components (activation sensors, safety beams, drive belt, hanger rollers, floor guide, and push switches). Over half of Hong Kong repair calls involve wear items or sensor drift rather than core motor failure.

When the motor and control board remain healthy, replacing worn rollers, belts, or sensors and re-commissioning travel limits restores reliable performance at a fraction of replacement cost.

  • Good candidates for repair: faulty sensors, loose belts, noisy rollers, or sticky exit switches.
  • Review replacement when: the motor overheats or stalls, or the control board resets unpredictably.
A short video with sound during a normal opening attempt helps distinguish roller wear from electrical faults.

2. Check the unit's age and how often it failed over the past year

High-traffic Hong Kong commercial operators typically deliver 7 to 10 years of steady service depending on daily cycles and maintenance. Beyond that window, failures tend to cascade — replacing a power module this month only to face a worn motor or ageing circuit board a few months later.

If the same door has broken down more than twice in 12 months, or if obsolete spare boards cost nearly half the price of a new mainstream operator, upgrading to a fresh drive unit with renewed warranty coverage usually saves money over the next few years.

  • Under 5 years old with a first-time fault: repair is almost always the right move.
  • 8–10+ years old with recurring breakdowns: compare an operator upgrade directly.
  • Discontinued models with scarce parts: upgrading removes long wait-time risks.
Two or three urgent call-outs on an ageing unit can quickly approach the cost of a clean operator upgrade.

3. Upgrading the operator does not mean tearing out your glass doors

Many business owners hesitate at the word 'replacement' because they picture days of hoarding, new glass fabrication, and shopfront disruption. In most cases, if your existing tempered glass panels and metal framing are intact, we retain them and upgrade only the overhead operator, track, hangers, and sensors.

This targeted upgrade preserves your existing shopfront appearance and can typically be completed within half a day to one day, or scheduled after business hours.

  • Sound tempered glass leaves and sturdy frames can usually stay in place.
  • Upgrading only the overhead drive kit saves significantly on glazing and finishing costs.
  • After-hours or weekend installation can be coordinated for busy retail locations.
A wide entrance photo lets our engineers check whether your current doors can be retained.

4. Review whether older anti-trap safety protection is still adequate

Older automatic doors installed over a decade ago often lack modern presence-detection safety curtains across the door threshold. Older visitors, children, or wheelchair users pausing briefly in the doorway can easily be struck if the sensor times out.

For clinics, residential clubhouses, commercial lobbies, and busy retail entrances, upgrading to modern anti-trap safety sensing and smooth speed control dramatically reduces incident risk.

  • Observe whether the door holds safely open when someone stands near the threshold.
  • Check for smooth deceleration rather than harsh end-of-travel impact.
  • Confirm any access-control or fire-interface requirements during an upgrade.
Modern presence-detection safety sensors are one of the highest-value improvements during a door upgrade.

5. Ask for an honest side-by-side repair vs upgrade view

The easiest way to avoid overspending is to share site photos and a brief fault clip with an experienced local Hong Kong team that handles both repairs and installations.

At Hwa Lai Engineering, we recommend targeted repair whenever it solves the problem reliably, and lay out a clear overhead-operator upgrade comparison whenever a system has genuinely reached end-of-life.

  • Mention approximate age and recent repair history if known.

Common questions

How long is the entrance closed if we only replace the overhead operator?

For a standard sliding glass door where the glass leaves and frame remain in place, replacing the overhead operator, track, and sensors typically takes half a day to one day. Off-peak or after-hours scheduling is available.

What if an on-site inspection reveals a deeper board or motor fault?

Remote photo review narrows down the likely cause, while on-site testing confirms internal electrical condition. We always explain the exact findings and confirm a clear quotation before replacing any parts.

Do upgraded operators include aftercare support?

Yes. Every installation or upgrade is commissioned and safety-tested by our Hong Kong technical team, with ongoing repair and planned maintenance support available.
