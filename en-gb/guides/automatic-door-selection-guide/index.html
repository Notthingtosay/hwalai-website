How to choose an automatic door for a Hong Kong shop

Start with the way the entrance will be used, then choose the operator, glass and safety configuration. Brand should not be the first question.

Prepared by Hwa Lai EngineeringUpdated 14 August 20267-minute read

The same operator can perform very differently at two entrances. Define the site conditions before choosing the system.

An automatic shop entrance is more than an operator fitted above a glass door. Opening width, daily footfall, door weight, wind exposure, access control and the way customers move through the entrance all affect the right configuration. A good selection sequence usually produces a more reliable and serviceable door.

Answer four questions before choosing an operator

01
Who uses the entrance?

Retail customers, office staff, clinic patients and goods deliveries require different opening speeds, clear widths and controls.

02
How busy is it each day?

A low-use doorway and an entrance running continuously through peak hours should not be assessed against the same duty requirement.

03
Is this a new door or a retrofit?

Before retaining existing glass doors, check their weight, support, frame, rail position and available travel space.

04
Do you need access control or power-failure operation?

Card access, keypads, mobile credentials, backup power, fire requirements and emergency egress should be considered at the design stage.

Choose the opening type before the brand

Automatic sliding door

The most common format for shops, offices and lobbies. The panels move sideways and keep the approach clear, but there must be enough space beside the opening to store the moving panels.

Telescopic sliding door

Where the frontage is restricted but a wider clear opening is needed, multiple panels can stack as they slide. The arrangement is more complex and must be designed around the opening.

Automatic swing operator

Some existing hinged doors can be automated without rebuilding the complete entrance. Door weight, opening direction, fixing structure and the swing path must still be checked.

A practical test:if an open door leaf would obstruct pedestrians, deliveries or an escape route, a swing system should not be chosen simply because it looks easier to install.

How should the operator be selected?

Brand is only one factor. Total door weight, opening width, daily operating cycles, movement speed, noise and the availability of parts and service support in Hong Kong matter just as much.

Typical shop entrance

The priorities are usually reliable movement, smooth operation and straightforward maintenance. A quiet site may not need the highest duty level, but safety sensing should not be omitted.

Busy lobby or long trading hours

Pay greater attention to continuous operation, load capacity, heat management, noise, parts support and planned maintenance—not only the product name.

Clinic, care facility or accessible entrance

Hold-open time, detection coverage, low-speed movement and anti-trap protection should be adjusted for users who may move more slowly.

How should glass and frames be specified?

Glass is not selected by thickness alone

Glass type, panel size, total weight, hardware cut-outs and location need to be considered together. Toughened and laminated glass are common options, but the final specification should follow the door design and site safety requirements.

Frameless glass doors

They give a clean, open shopfront, but glass fittings, floor level, panel stability and finishing details must be accurate.

Aluminium or stainless-steel frames

Framed doors can make it easier to integrate seals, locks and larger panels. Aluminium or 304 stainless steel may suit many indoor locations; coastal, wet or corrosive sites should be assessed for a more corrosion-resistant specification.

Which safety features should not be overlooked?

An activation sensor tells the door that somebody is approaching. A safety sensor protects the closing and travel area. They perform different jobs, so a basic approach sensor should not be treated as complete anti-trap protection.

Open
Activation sensing

Adjust the detection field for the direction and speed of traffic to reduce false opening and late response.

Safe
Door-area safety sensing

Monitor the moving and closing area, particularly where children, older people or trolleys use the entrance.

Power
Backup power and egress

Confirm whether the door should unlock, open, remain closed or allow manual passage during a power failure, in line with site and fire requirements.

What should you prepare for the first enquiry?

You do not need to identify the operator model yourself. The following is normally enough for an initial assessment:

Take four photographs:a straight-on view of the entrance, the head or proposed rail position, both sides of the opening and the nearest power point. Add approximate opening width, height, preferred movement and the type of premises.

For an existing faulty automatic door, also photograph the operator label and control indicators, then record a short opening-and-closing video with sound. This helps separate sensor, door, power and operator problems.

Common questions

Does automation always require a complete new glass entrance?

No. Some existing door sets can be retained, but their weight, structure, rail space and glass fittings must first be confirmed as suitable for powered operation.

Are frameless glass doors right for every shop?

No. A wide opening, wind exposure, sealing, locking or intensive use may make a framed door the better option.

Does an entrance sensor mean the door cannot trap someone?

Not necessarily. Activation sensing and door-area anti-trap safety perform different functions and should be specified for the chosen door type and users.

Can an automatic door connect to card or mobile access?

Usually, yes. The control logic, locking, request-to-exit device, fire interface and power-failure behaviour should be agreed before installation.
