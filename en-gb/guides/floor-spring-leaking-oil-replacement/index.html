Glass door floor spring leaking oil or sagging? Hong Kong guide

When a shopfront or office glass door starts leaking oil, dropping out of alignment, or slamming shut, it creates both an entry nuisance and a safety hazard. Here are five practical signs to check before booking a replacement.

Minor speed drift or slight misalignment can sometimes be tuned on site. However, visible oil around the cover plate, sudden slamming, or a door dragging along the floor usually means the internal seals have failed and the floor spring should be replaced.

Across Hong Kong shops, commercial lobbies, and offices, heavy tempered glass doors rely on concealed floor springs (floor hinges) to carry the leaf weight and control closing speed. High daily footfall, floor mopping, and dust gradually wear internal seals and pivots. Knowing what to observe helps you decide quickly between a simple adjustment and a full unit replacement.

1. Why oil leaks and slamming doors need prompt attention

A floor spring uses hydraulic oil to cushion the closing sweep and the final latching zone. When seals age, dark oil begins seeping around the stainless cover plate. As fluid escapes, damping disappears and the glass door snaps shut forcefully, risking pinched fingers for customers, children, or elderly visitors.

Repeated hard slamming also sends shock loads straight into the corners of tempered glass panels and patch fittings. Many sudden glass door breakages in retail shopfronts trace back to an ignored leaking floor spring left unserviced for months.

  • Check around the stainless floor cover plate for dark oily residue.
  • Watch whether the final closing sweep cushions smoothly or slams hard.
  • Warn users and arrange inspection promptly once damping is lost.
Oil leakage indicates worn internal seals; turning speed valves cannot restore lost hydraulic fluid.

2. What causes a glass door to sag or scrape the floor?

Paired glass doors often drift out of level, narrowing the centre gap until the bottom edge rubs against tiles or marble. While a worn floor spring spindle is a common culprit, loose top patch fittings, worn top pivots, or a loosened floor cement box can produce similar symptoms.

Because a single tempered glass leaf can weigh well over 50 kg, never loosen patch-fitting bolts or wedge metal strips under the door yourself. Safe servicing requires experienced technicians with glass suction lifters to unmount the door cleanly, inspect the cement box, and re-align both upper and lower hardware.

  • Stand back and check whether the top line and centre gap remain parallel.
  • Note any scraping marks on the floor or stiffness at specific angles.
  • Do not loosen top or bottom glass clamps without proper glass lifting support.
A straight-on photo showing the full frame and centre gap quickly reveals how far the door has dropped.

3. Lost 90-degree hold-open function: wear or wrong spec?

Retail stores and clinics often rely on a 90-degree hold-open position during deliveries or busy hours. If a door that previously held open now swings straight back, the internal hold-open cam or spindle alignment is typically worn.

Before replacing the unit, confirm whether your entrance requires 90-degree hold-open or non-hold-open self-closing action, and whether the door swings one way or both ways. Matching the right specification upfront avoids daily inconvenience after installation.

  • Confirm whether you need 90-degree hold-open or always-closing action.
  • Note whether the entrance swings inwards, outwards, or double-action.
  • Mention if the floor area stays damp from frequent washing or rain.
Confirming swing direction and hold-open preference before ordering ensures smooth daily operation.

4. Can popular brands like Dorma, GMT, or Crown be swapped directly?

Hong Kong commercial sites commonly use Dorma (Dormakaba), GMT, Crown, and other mainstream heavy-duty floor springs. Cement box dimensions vary across series, so if your existing floor box is still solid and rust-free, matching a compatible unit often allows direct replacement without cutting the surrounding floor tiles.

If the existing cement box has corroded loose or the heavy glass leaf needs a higher load rating, our technicians will advise clearly whether floor cutting and re-grouting are required. Hwa Lai Engineering supplies and services all popular mainstream floor spring and glass hardware brands.

  • Photograph the stainless floor cover plate clearly so brand markings or screw layout are visible.
  • Share approximate door height, width, and glass thickness if known.
  • Let us know if off-peak or after-hours scheduling is required by building management.
When the existing cement box is sound, direct unit replacement minimises downtime and tile disturbance.

Common questions

How long does a floor spring replacement take in Hong Kong?

If the existing cement box is intact and requires no concrete cutting, replacing a single floor spring and re-aligning the glass door typically takes around 1 to 2 hours. After-hours or off-peak scheduling can be arranged for busy shops and lobbies.

Can a fast-closing glass door be adjusted instead of replaced?

If there is no oil leak and the door has not sagged significantly, adjusting the speed valves may restore smooth closing. Once oil has leaked out, replacement is the safe and lasting solution.

Do I need to buy the replacement floor spring myself?

No. We supply and install popular mainstream brands including Dorma, GMT, Crown, and compatible heavy-duty models matched to your door weight and existing box size.
